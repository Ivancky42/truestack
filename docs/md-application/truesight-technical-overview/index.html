Problem

1 — Problem

Credit decisions still rely on delayed and incomplete information.

Malaysian lenders and financing operators typically assess a borrower from a bureau extract, supporting documents and records in the loan or pembiayaan system. That view is often days or weeks old by the time a credit committee sits. It also rarely shows whether the same person or company is already stretched on another book.

The same limitation applies to Shariah financing. Affordability and cash flow remain material. What must not occur is treating deferred profit as interest, combining Ta'widh with Gharamah, or running a Shariah book through the same risk engine and infrastructure as a conventional licence. If a Shariah committee cannot see why a customer was approved, it is being asked to rely on assertion rather than evidence.

Point-in-time bureau scores

CCRIS or CTOS extracts describe a past position. Income, utilisation and other-lender activity can change between the pull and the decision.

Thin-file customers

SMEs, the self-employed and younger applicants often have limited bureau history. Officers then either decline or improvise, because the model only reads a credit file.

Inconsistent judgement

Without a documented method, the same pack produces different limits depending on the officer. Subsequent audit sees the outcome, not the reasoning.

Late identification of stress

Falling balances, rising commitments and missed instalments are often noticed in collections, not at origination or during scheduled monitoring.

Islamic Digital Economy

Why a conventional scorecard is not sufficient for a Shariah book

A conventional engine looks for interest, late interest and a single arrears field. A Tawarruq facility has a deferred sale price, a profit margin already approved in the Aqad, and two late-charge ledgers that must remain separate: Ta'widh (compensation, capped) and Gharamah (penalty, payable to charity). If the model cannot distinguish those fields, the operator cannot demonstrate that risk assessment remained within the approved contract. TrueSight is designed so that it can.

Solution

1 — Proposed solution

TrueSight is the decision layer. The officer remains accountable.

TrueSight is Truestack’s proprietary AI service for borrower and customer creditworthiness. It consumes data the institution is entitled to use, applies rules and calibrated models, and returns a score, a probability of default, the principal reasons, and a recommended next step. After a facility is booked, the same engine re-scores the account on a schedule.

The licensed lender or Shariah financing operator remains the decision-maker. TrueSight does not originate, disburse or collect. It does not issue a fatwa, approve an Aqad, or execute a commodity trade. Those functions remain with the operator, the committee, and TrueSyariah / TrueCommodity where they apply. Adjacent Truestack products (TrueIdentity, document extraction, AML screening) are treated here as data sources, not as separate Malaysia Digital activities.

KPKT-licensed money lenders, fintech credit companies and digital lenders on TrueKredit or a third-party loan system. Interest-bearing products, debt-service ratios, utilisation, bureau and bank data.

Modules

2 — Modules

Nine software functions in one service.

Each item below is a software module. Officers use it in the console; other systems call it through an API. None of these items is a workshop, campaign or advisory engagement.

1. Data intelligence

Normalise bureau extracts, bank and transaction files, LMS or TrueSyariah records, and statements into a feature table. Network matching uses consented hashed identifiers (NRIC or SSM). Raw loan files are not pooled.

2. Financial health

Income stability, expense pattern, cash-flow volatility, debt service, utilisation and recent deterioration. On a Shariah book the engine uses deferred profit and instalments on the sale price.

3. Credit risk engine

Supervised models (gradient-boosted trees) produce a TrueSight score, probability of default over defined horizons, and inputs to expected loss. Example output: score 78/100, PD 4.2%, suggested facility RM50,000.

4. Explainable AI

Ranked factors that moved the score, written so a credit officer, internal auditor or Shariah committee member can follow the result without a data-science background.

5. Decision support

Maps the score to approve, conditions, manual review or decline, plus amount and tenure. Conventional mode may suggest risk-based pricing. Shariah mode does not invent a profit rate; that remains inside the approved Aqad.

6. Early-warning monitor

Re-scores booked accounts when new permitted data arrives. On Shariah books, Ta'widh and Gharamah are separate signals, not a single late-interest flag.

7. Portfolio intelligence

Book-level PD and expected loss, grade mix, concentration, vintage and watchlist — produced from that book’s own tenancy.

8. Consented network signals

Optional. A hashed identity may be matched only against participating books of the same type. Cross-type matching between conventional and Shariah is not performed.

9. Shariah financing mode

The same engine, deployed only on the operator’s dedicated TrueSyariah AWS Malaysia tenancy. Feature dictionary includes deferred sale price, profit margin, Ta'widh outstanding, Gharamah outstanding (charity, not income) and commodity-trade completion flags from TrueCommodity where present. TrueSight scores the customer and the facility; it does not execute Tawarruq or rule on contracts.

Architecture

2 — System architecture

The assessment runs in the existing loan file. Results are written back with a model version.

Presentation layer

Credit users work in a web console (React, Next.js, TypeScript): borrower 360°, score and PD, explainability panel, recommendation, early-warning queue and portfolio boards. On TrueKredit the panel is embedded on the borrower and application screens. On TrueSyariah it sits on the facility file, with pembiayaan labels. A third-party loan system may call the API and never open the Truestack console.

Application and data

Node.js services receive HTTPS requests. Data is validated and stored in that client’s isolated PostgreSQL database on AWS Malaysia; Redis holds short-lived feature lookups. A feature pipeline builds the model table. Inference returns score, PD, explanations and a recommendation. Every call is audit-logged: requester, time, model identifier and book type.

Request sequence

  1. The loan or pembiayaan system (TrueKredit, TrueSyariah, or another LMS) submits identifiers and permitted fields, or the officer works in the console.
  2. Ingestion verifies consent flags and book type. A Shariah request directed at a conventional tenancy is refused, and the reverse.
  3. Features are built for that book type. Hashed network lookup, if enabled, runs only against participating books of the same type.
  4. Rules and classical models produce the score. An LLM, where used, writes the narrative from a bounded case context — it does not set the PD.
  5. The payload is stored against the file and returned. Scheduled jobs re-score booked accounts using only data still covered by a valid purpose.

Cloud hosting

Amazon Web Services, Malaysia region. Truestack hosts the TrueSight inference service, including the AWS Bedrock model gateway, in Malaysia. Each client’s borrower and facility data remains on that client’s dedicated AWS Malaysia account — the same pattern used for TrueKredit and TrueSyariah. A Shariah operator is never placed on a tenancy that also holds conventional loans. Hashed network features, where used, are derived signals, not a copy of another lender’s file.

Data residency

Personal data is stored in Malaysia. Encryption in transit and at rest. Backups remain on the client tenancy.

Isolation

One AWS account, database and secrets vault per operator. The Shariah ring-fence is architectural, not a query filter.

Audit

Model version, feature snapshot and explanation are stored with the decision and can be reconstructed for committee or regulatory review.

Decision flow

2 — Decision flow

Capture, normalise, evaluate, decide, monitor. Each stage has its own controls.

Separating these stages allows the operator to control cost, explainability and the information an LLM is permitted to see. Identity, documents, AML and external signals feed TrueSight; they are not presented as separate qualifying activities in this application.

CaptureApplicant, documents, bureau, company and optional partner signals — only with a recorded purpose.
→
NormaliseExtracted facts, entity match, validation, feature store. Lineage and confidence on every field.
→
EvaluateRules and scorecards first. Classical ML for PD. LLM for narrative and exceptions only.
→
DecideApprove, decline or review. Evidence pack, policy version, officer override where required.
→
MonitorWatchlist refresh, portfolio drift, outcomes written back for subsequent training.

Control plane (applies to every stage)

Consent and PDPA purpose limitation · data classification · model routing (which Bedrock model, which region) · human-review thresholds · prompt and inference logs · retention and deletion. Ambiguous or high-risk external signals are routed to an officer. They are not silently absorbed into the grade.

What is permitted to predict risk

Probability of default, affordability and fraud flags are produced by rules, scorecards and gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost / LightGBM). Those models are measurable, calibrated and versioned. A generative model does not own the credit score.

Role of the language model

Normalising extracted fields, drafting the reasons an officer will record, flagging inconsistencies, and assembling the evidence pack. If the language model is unavailable, scoring still runs. A narrative without a score is not a decision.

On a Shariah tenancy the same five stages apply. The feature dictionary and the decision outputs change; the control plane does not. Book type is checked before any Bedrock call. A conventional prompt template cannot receive a Shariah case, and the reverse.

Data privacy

3 — Data privacy

Personal data is processed only for a stated credit or financing purpose.

TrueSight is designed around the Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (as amended), purpose limitation, and Malaysian data residency. Additional data — including any future telco, e-commerce or BNPL feed — is not connected until a lawful basis, a processing agreement and a data-protection impact assessment are in place. Truestack does not sell personal data and does not train foundation models on borrower files.

Lawful basis and consent

Each source is tagged with origin, timestamp, confidence, collection basis and retention rule. Sensitive or third-party data is collected only after a clear purpose statement. The applicant may access, correct or request deletion where the PDPA so requires.

Data minimisation

The platform stores features required for the decision, not a copy of a partner’s entire customer database. Call content, precise location trails, private messages and browsing histories are not in scope. Preference is given to derived indicators (for example payment regularity) over raw event logs.

Residency and isolation

Production personal data, extracted documents, logs and backups are held in the AWS Malaysia region on the operator’s dedicated tenancy. Conventional and Shariah books do not share a database. Cross-border model calls, if ever required, use redacted payloads and customer approval, or wait for an in-region model.

Explainability and fairness

Every field used in a score or a decline must have a plain-language explanation path. Web, social or alternative-data signals are not used as hidden proxies for protected characteristics. Ambiguous signals default to human review.

Processors and models

AWS is the cloud and Bedrock processor. Borrower PII is not used to fine-tune Bedrock models. Prompt and output logs are retained or deleted under the client’s retention schedule. Open-weight or private endpoints may be used for higher-sensitivity workloads.

Regulated customers

For Bank Negara-regulated institutions, the design supports outsourcing and RMiT expectations: data-location records, access control, incident notification, audit logs and the ability to reconstruct a decision. Operational assurance (encryption, key management, vulnerability management) is part of the control plane, not an afterthought.

Hierarchy of sources. Official records and customer-provided evidence take precedence over partner or web content. A telco, marketplace or BNPL signal cannot override a court or SSM record, and cannot enter the score unless the operator can explain it to the applicant and to an auditor.

Artificial intelligence

4 — Artificial intelligence

Classical models produce the score. AWS Bedrock is used for language, with cost-aware routing.

AWS Bedrock is the managed model layer. Business logic is not hard-wired to a single provider or model. A Model Gateway selects the route, logs the call and can fail over. The cheapest model that meets the quality threshold for that task is preferred.

Model Gateway

Single interface for routing, cost controls, evaluation and audit. Malaysia-region models are preferred. If a required model is unavailable in-region, only redacted or customer-approved payloads may leave, or the job waits for an in-region alternative.

Prohibited uses

No fine-tuning of Bedrock models on borrower PII. No generative model setting the probability of default. No Shariah case sent to a conventional prompt template, or the reverse.

TaskTypical routeRationale
Document extraction Specialist OCR / document AI first; LLM second pass (GLM Flash or Amazon Nova Lite class on Bedrock). High volume. Deterministic extraction is cheaper; the LLM only normalises fields and flags contradictions.
Transaction classification Nova Lite / GLM Flash class, tested on labelled Malaysian statements. Repetitive work where cost and Bahasa Malaysia / English coverage matter.
Risk narrative and exceptions Claude, GLM or Nova Pro class via Bedrock. Requires structured output and a paragraph an officer can defend. Receives a bounded case context, not a raw document dump.
Public diligence (optional) Search tools plus a research-class model only where contracted. Adverse-media discovery is separated from the score. A reviewer must dispose the hit.
PD, affordability, fraud probability Scorecards and XGBoost / LightGBM on the feature store. Not Bedrock. Must be calibrated, monitored for drift, and replayable from a model version identifier.

Training

Scorecards and tree models are trained, and periodically retrained, on labelled Malaysian outcomes: defaults, arrears, fraud flags and officer overrides. Labels remain on the operator’s tenancy unless a separate, de-identified pooling agreement exists. The long-term advantage is a Malaysia-specific corpus (local forms, naming, SMEs, informal income), not a particular large language model.

Memory

There is no conversational memory that carries one borrower into another tenant. Working memory is the risk data layer: the case file, the feature store, consent evidence, and the outcome written back after the facility performs. The LLM sees only the facts assembled for that request. Prompts, model identifiers, token counts and outputs are logged, then retained or deleted under the client’s PDPA schedule.

Distribution

5 — How TrueSight reaches the market

It ships inside TrueKredit, and it is also opened as an API.

A standalone scoring tool has to win a new workflow. TrueSight does not. Licensed lenders already run their books on TrueKredit — origination, schedules, collections and KPKT paperwork, on their own AWS Malaysia tenancy. TrueSight is built to sit in that file. Operators who do not use TrueKredit can call the same engine through an API and keep the system they already have.

Channel 1

Native in TrueKredit

TrueSight is embedded on the borrower and application screens of TrueKredit, which is already in production with licensed Malaysian money lenders. The officer does not open a second system. The score, PD, reasons and recommendation are written onto the same record as the loan, the repayment schedule and the Lampiran. Isolation, audit logs and Malaysia residency are the ones that tenancy already uses.

Because the LMS already holds applications, commitments, disbursements and arrears, TrueSight is not scoring from an empty payload. That is the practical advantage over a vendor who only receives a bureau extract through a new integration.

Channel 2

Open API

The same TrueSight engine is exposed as an HTTPS API for banks, fintechs, credit companies and operators whose loan or pembiayaan system is not TrueKredit. They submit permitted fields, receive score, PD, explanations and a recommended next step, and write the result into their own file. They do not have to move the loan book.

The control plane does not change: consent and purpose, book-type lock, dedicated AWS Malaysia tenancy, model-version logging, and the same prohibition on mixing conventional and Shariah data. The API is a delivery method, not a second product with weaker controls.

Islamic Digital Economy

TrueSyariah operators use the same two channels

On TrueSyariah, TrueSight is embedded on the facility file, with pembiayaan labels and no interest field. A Shariah operator on another platform may call the API in Shariah mode. In both cases the request is served only from that operator’s dedicated TrueSyariah tenancy. The API will refuse a Shariah payload pointed at a conventional endpoint.

What this means for the activity. Distribution through an LMS that is already live shortens time-to-use for existing clients. The API extends the same proprietary engine to institutions that will never replace their core system. Both paths are software licensing and usage of TrueSight — not consulting, not a bureau, and not a requirement that the customer abandon their current origination stack.

Conventional setting

Apex Credit Sdn. Bhd. (fictional) is a KPKT-licensed money lender with approximately 1,800 live personal loans on TrueKredit. The officer opens Tech Solutions Sdn. Bhd., SSM 202201012345, manufacturing, incorporated 2017.

Shariah setting

The same engine, on a separate tenancy, would support a TrueSyariah operator assessing a Tawarruq working-capital facility. Profit terms would already sit in the approved Aqad. TrueSight would not propose an interest rate; it would address amount, tenure and whether the file should go to review.

Sequence on the conventional desk

  1. SSM lookup is in order. Papers in the file show no obvious litigation. A bureau-only read would likely support approval at a mid five-figure limit.
  2. TrueSight Preview runs as the officer enters the SSM number. Hashed match: already borrowing from one other participating lender. Name matches; telephone number does not.
  3. Exposure band RM500,000–RM1,000,000. On-time band 0–10%. Activity counts and recency as shown on page 11. Risk band: high.
  4. Recommendation: manual review; cap new exposure; request current bank statements; reconcile the telephone discrepancy before disbursement. No telco, e-commerce or BNPL feed is required for this conclusion.
  5. If a smaller facility is later booked, monitoring continues on permitted features only. A further rise in utilisation raises an early-warning item rather than waiting for a missed instalment.

Without TrueSight

The officer sees a manufacturer with an SSM number and a complete form. Concentration at other lenders is not visible. Contact-data inconsistency is easy to miss. The committee pack contains a score without supporting reasons.

With TrueSight

The officer still decides. The file now carries a documented high-risk band, a network flag and a list of factors. That record is the audit trail. On a Shariah desk, it is what the committee can sample.

This example does not claim a reduction in default rates. TrueSight has been in development since Q2 2026 and is not generally released. It is included so that the method is concrete for assessment of this application.
